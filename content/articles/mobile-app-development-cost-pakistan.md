"How much will my app cost?" is the first question almost every client asks us, and the honest answer is always "it depends". That answer is frustrating, so this guide explains exactly what it depends on. By the end you should be able to estimate whether your idea is a small, medium or large project, understand the running costs most people forget, and ask developers the right questions.

We build and run our own apps, including a short-video and LIVE streaming app on Android and iOS, so the points below come from paying these costs ourselves.

## The biggest factor: what the app actually does

Two apps can look similar on screen and be very different underneath. Cost follows the work hidden behind each feature.

**Simple apps** show information and collect a little input. Think of a restaurant menu, a company catalogue, a booking request form or a news reader. There is a small number of screens, a basic admin panel and little logic on the server.

**Medium apps** have user accounts and real data. Examples are a marketplace with listings and chat, a delivery app with order tracking, or a learning app with progress and payments. Each of these needs a proper backend, a database, security rules and an admin dashboard.

**Complex apps** involve real-time features, money or heavy media. LIVE video, voice and video calling, wallets and withdrawals, multi-vendor payments, maps with live tracking, or anything that must handle thousands of users at the same moment. These need careful engineering, testing on many devices and ongoing monitoring.

Before asking for a quote, write down every feature as a simple sentence: "A user can sign up with a phone number", "A seller can upload up to ten photos", "An admin can approve withdrawals". This list is the single most useful document you can give a developer.

## Android, iOS or both

In Pakistan most users are on Android, so many businesses start there. If your customers are abroad, or you target higher-income users, iOS matters more.

- **One platform first** is the cheapest way to launch and learn what users really want.
- **Both platforms natively** (separate Android and iOS apps) gives the best performance and feel, but costs more because much of the work is done twice.
- **Cross-platform frameworks** share one codebase across both. They can save money for many business apps, but apps with heavy video, camera or real-time features often still need native work.

There is also a hardware cost for iOS: building and publishing iPhone apps requires a Mac, or a paid cloud build service.

## Design

A clean design built from standard components costs less than a fully custom look with illustrations and animations. Good design is not decoration; clear screens reduce support requests and help users finish what they came to do. Ask whether design is included in a quote or charged separately.

## The backend nobody sees

Most of the cost of a medium or complex app sits on the server side: user accounts, databases, file storage, notifications, payment verification and the admin panel. Cloud platforms such as Firebase let small teams build this faster, but someone still has to design the data, write security rules and make sure one user can never read another user's private information.

If a quote looks surprisingly cheap, check whether the backend and admin panel are included at all.

## Running costs after launch

The development quote is not the total cost. Plan a monthly budget for these from day one.

- **Store accounts.** Google Play charges a one-time registration fee of 25 US dollars. Apple's Developer Program costs 99 US dollars per year.
- **Servers and cloud services.** Small apps can often start within free tiers. Costs grow with users, stored files and video traffic. Media-heavy apps cost the most to run.
- **Third-party services.** SMS for phone login, email delivery, maps, real-time video minutes, payment gateways and analytics may each have their own pricing once you pass free limits.
- **Store commission.** Digital goods sold inside apps through Google Play or the App Store pay a commission to the store. Factor this into your prices.
- **Maintenance.** Android and iOS change every year, and stores require apps to keep up with new rules and target versions. Budget for regular updates and bug fixes, not just new features.

## Timeline

Time and cost move together. A simple app can be ready in a few weeks. A medium app usually takes a few months. Complex apps with real-time features are typically built in phases, launching a core version first and adding features based on real usage. Be cautious of anyone who promises a complex app in a very short time; the testing and security work is usually what gets cut.

## How to get an accurate quote

1. **Write your feature list** in plain sentences, as described above.
2. **Mark what is essential for launch** and what can wait for version two.
3. **Share examples** of apps you like, and say what you like about each one.
4. **Say which platforms** you need at launch.
5. **Ask what is included:** design, backend, admin panel, store publishing, testing, and how many months of support after launch.
6. **Ask who owns the code** and the store accounts. You should.

A good developer will ask you questions back. If someone gives a fixed price within minutes without understanding your features, the price will probably change later.

## Ways to save money without cutting quality

- **Launch with fewer features.** A focused first version reaches users sooner and shows you what to build next.
- **Use proven services** for login, payments and notifications instead of building them from scratch.
- **Start on one platform** if most of your users are there.
- **Keep the admin panel simple** at first; it only needs to do what your team does every day.
