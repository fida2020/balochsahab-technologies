Safety & Security

Online Safety for Creators and Live Streamers

By Baloch Sahab Technologies team · 26 October 2026 · 6 min read

Sharing your life and work online can be rewarding, but it also exposes you to strangers. Most viewers are friendly. A small number are not, and some will go to surprising lengths to find out where you live, harass you or trick you out of your accounts. The good news is that a few habits remove most of the risk.

As the team behind a short-video and LIVE app, we see safety from the platform side: reports, blocks, moderation tools and the patterns that scammers repeat. This guide turns that into practical advice for creators, whatever app you use.

Protect your location and personal details

The most serious risks start with someone working out who you are offline. Very few people announce their address. They leak it through small details that add up.

Check your background

Before you film, look at what is visible behind you. Common giveaways include:

  • Windows with a recognisable view, street signs, shop names or landmarks
  • House numbers, car number plates and your building entrance
  • Letters, parcels, bills, ID cards and school or office badges
  • Uniforms and logos that identify your school or workplace

Tidy these out of shot, or film facing a plain wall. Blur anything you notice later in editing before posting.

Watch the timing and routine

Posting from a café while you are still there tells viewers exactly where to find you. Post after you have left. Avoid showing a predictable routine, such as the same gym at the same time every day. If you travel, consider sharing the trip after you return.

Remove photo and video metadata

Photos and videos taken on a phone can contain location data (often called EXIF or GPS metadata). Many social apps strip this on upload, but files you send directly, by email, messaging apps or file sharing may keep it. Switch off location tagging for the camera in your phone settings if you do not need it, and use a metadata-removal option before sharing original files.

Separate your creator identity

Use a dedicated email address for your creator accounts and business enquiries. Do not reuse your personal phone number publicly. If you work with brands, consider a business address service or a post office box instead of your home address. Keep family members, especially children, out of identifiable content unless you have thought carefully about it.

Handle harassment with the tools you have

Every major platform gives you tools to control who can reach you. Learn them before you need them.

  • Block removes a person from your space. Blocking is not rude; it is maintenance.
  • Mute or restrict hides someone's comments from others without telling them, which can avoid escalation.
  • Keyword filters automatically hide comments containing words you choose. Add slurs, your real name, your neighbourhood and any phrases trolls repeat.
  • Comment and message controls let you limit who can comment, duet, mention you or message you, for example only followers or only people you follow.
  • Report sends content to the platform's safety team. Report threats, hate and sexual harassment even if you also block, because it helps the platform act against repeat offenders.

When something serious happens, such as a credible threat or someone sharing your private information, take screenshots including usernames and dates before you block, so you have evidence. In cases of threats to your safety, contact local authorities as well as the platform.

Live streams carry extra risk

Live video is different from recorded video because you cannot edit it. Whatever is on camera goes out instantly, and the chat moves faster than you can read it.

  1. Do a background check before going live, just as you would for a video, and check what the camera sees when you move around.
  2. Appoint moderators. Trusted friends or regular viewers who can mute, remove and report people in your chat take a huge load off you. Agree in advance what they should remove.
  3. Set chat filters and slow mode where available, so spam and abuse are caught before you see it.
  4. Be careful with guests. When you invite a viewer to join your stream, you are giving a stranger your audience. Many apps let you mute or remove a guest instantly; know where that button is.
  5. Never reveal live location. Avoid streaming outdoors in places that can be identified, and do not say where you are going next.
  6. Have an exit plan. If a stream turns bad, it is fine to end it. You owe nobody an explanation.

Viewers sometimes try to push streamers into risky behaviour, or offer gifts in exchange for something you are not comfortable doing. You set the boundaries of your stream, not the chat.

Scams that target creators

Creators are attractive targets because their accounts have audiences, and losing one is painful. Scammers know this, and their messages are designed to make you panic or get excited so you act quickly.

Fake copyright or policy warnings

A message says your account will be deleted for a copyright or community-guidelines violation unless you "verify" through a link. The link leads to a fake login page that steals your password and two-factor code. Real platforms communicate through official in-app notifications and their verified email domains. Never log in through a link sent in a direct message; open the app yourself and check your account status there.

Fake brand deals

An exciting sponsorship offer arrives, then the "brand" asks you to download a file to see the contract or product brief, log in to a portal, or pay a fee to join. Downloaded files can contain malware that steals your logged-in sessions. Check that the email domain matches the real company, look up the company independently, and never pay to receive a sponsorship.

Verification and badge scams

Offers to sell you a verification badge or "unlock" monetisation features are scams. Badges and features are granted by the platform itself.

Account recovery takeovers

Someone claiming to be a friend says they accidentally sent a login code to your number and asks you to forward it. That code is for your account. Never share any code you receive. Strong account security makes all of these scams much less effective; our guide to protecting your accounts with 2FA and passkeys walks through it.

Look after yourself

Safety is not only about attackers. Constant comments, criticism and the pressure to keep posting can wear anyone down.

  • Take breaks from reading comments. Ask a moderator to skim them for anything important.
  • Decide in advance what topics you will not discuss publicly, such as relationships, family or health.
  • Remember that a loud minority in the comments is not your whole audience.
  • Talk to people you trust offline when something bothers you. Many creators experience harassment; you are not alone and it is not your fault.

Conclusion

Online safety comes down to controlling what strangers can learn about you and who can reach you. Check your background, delay location-revealing posts, strip metadata, learn your block, mute and filter tools, use moderators on live streams and treat urgent messages with suspicion. Set these habits up once, review them every few months, and you can focus on creating with much more peace of mind.
